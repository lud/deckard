# Presentation-craft knowledge — source survey

Research groundwork (2026-07-27) for a future Deckard milestone: packing
presentation-craft knowledge into the plugin (authoring guidance the plan and
media skills can lean on). This doc records *where* the good knowledge lives,
who owns it, and what can legally be reused; distillation into shipped
reference docs is the later step. All facts were verified against live pages
on the date above; URLs inline.

## "Death by PowerPoint" — two famous works share the name

1. **The TEDx talk** (the one usually meant): **"How to avoid death By
   PowerPoint"** — **David JP Phillips**, TEDxStockholmSalon, published
   2014-04-14, ~20 min.
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Iwpi1Lm6dFo
   Five research-motivated slide-design rules: one message per slide; never
   put spoken sentences as on-screen text (the redundancy effect kills
   retention); make the most important element the biggest; use contrast to
   steer attention; at most **six objects per slide**; bonus — dark
   backgrounds keep focus on the speaker.
2. **The SlideShare deck** (older, also famous): **"Death by PowerPoint (and
   how to fight it)"** — **Alexei Kapterev**, 2007, 1M+ views.
   https://www.kapterev.com/media/Death.by.PowerPoint.eng.pdf
   Framework: significance, structure, simplicity, rehearsal. Later expanded
   into *Presentation Secrets* (Wiley, 2011).

The phrase itself is a generic idiom older than both works.

## Evidence-based sources (experimentally backed — best for encoding rules)

- **Richard E. Mayer — *Multimedia Learning*** (CUP; 3rd ed. 2021, **15
  principles** from 200+ controlled studies). For slides especially:
  coherence (cut extraneous material), redundancy (don't display text
  identical to narration — the same effect Phillips cites), signaling,
  contiguity, segmenting, modality. The books are copyrighted but the
  numbered principles are standard science, freely restatable with citation
  (summaries: https://educationaltechnology.net/mayers-principles-of-multimedia-learning/).
- **Michael Alley — assertion-evidence structure** (*The Craft of Scientific
  Presentations*, Springer). Full-sentence **assertion headline** + **visual
  evidence** instead of topic-phrase + bullets; comprehension gains shown in
  studies; NSF-funded. https://www.assertion-evidence.com/ (Penn State)
  offers **free** tutorials, templates and model talks intended for
  adoption.

## Licensing map

- **TED/TEDx talks**: CC **BY-NC-ND** 4.0
  (https://www.ted.com/about/our-organization/our-policies-terms/ted-talks-usage-policy)
  — link and attribute freely; no commercial redistribution of the
  video/transcript, no derivatives. Distilling the *ideas* (not
  copyrightable) with citation is fine. Applies to all four Phillips talks.
- **assertion-evidence.com**: freely downloadable, meant for adoption —
  friendliest source for direct structural reuse (check per-file terms
  before literal redistribution).
- **Duarte free ebooks / Reynolds tip pages**: free to read, still
  copyrighted (Duarte's titles are ® marks) — distill and cite, don't copy
  text or use the trademarked names in product copy.
- **Mayer's principles**: restate the science with citation.
- **All paid books** (Phillips, Kapterev, Tufte, Minto, Heath, Gallo,
  Anderson, Duarte): distill principles, cite, never copy text.

## Practical takeaway for Deckard

The freely usable backbone for a knowledge pack:

1. **Mayer's principles** restated as science — the strongest ground for
   *rules* (they overlap heavily with Phillips' five, giving the rules an
   evidence citation rather than an opinion one).
2. **Assertion-evidence** — a concrete, adoptable slide *structure* that
   maps naturally onto the §2 format (assertion = slide title, evidence =
   body/media) and onto the companion-slide pattern.
3. **Phillips' five rules** + the other three talks — distilled with
   attribution and links.
4. **Duarte's sparkline / Anderson's throughline** — narrative-level
   guidance for `/deck:ideate` and `/deck:plan`, distilled.
5. **Reynolds' tip pages and Duarte's free ebooks** — supporting guidance,
   distilled with citation.

Likely shape when the milestone lands: `reference/` docs in the plugin
(loaded on demand by plan/media), each rule carrying its source — slide-level
rules feeding `/deck:plan`'s authoring and `/deck:media`'s proposals,
narrative-level guidance feeding `/deck:ideate`.
